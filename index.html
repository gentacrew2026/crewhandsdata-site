Scope of work

Twelve tasks across two divisions, recorded on live jobsites and in occupied buildings. Open any line item to see what the camera captures and what a model can learn from it.

03

Active construction site

5 line items
ItemTaskTools in frame
03.01Site cleanup & debris removalBroom, shovel, wheelbarrow, bins+

What we record

Sweeping, sorting scrap by material, loading bins and wheelbarrows, clearing walkways between active work zones.

What a model learns

Grasping irregular, unknown objects and moving through clutter that changes every hour.

Site conditions

Dust, uneven ground, mixed debris, other crews working nearby.

03.02General skilled tradesTape, square, saw, drill driver, level+

What we record

Measuring, marking, cutting and fastening: framing, blocking, setting fixtures and checking for level and plumb.

What a model learns

Measure, mark, cut sequences and frequent tool switching within a single task.

Site conditions

Power tools, sawdust, work at bench and at height.

03.03Drywall hanging, taping & muddingScrew gun, taping knife, mud pan, sander+

What we record

Lifting and positioning sheets, driving screws, bedding tape, applying and feathering joint compound, sanding.

What a model learns

Handling large rigid panels, then spreading a deformable material to a smooth finish.

Site conditions

Fine dust, overhead work, bright and flat lighting that hides surface defects.

03.04Insulation installationUtility knife, stapler, straightedge+

What we record

Measuring stud bays, cutting batts, fitting them around boxes and pipes, stapling vapor barrier.

What a model learns

Manipulating soft, compressible material and fitting it into tight cavities without gaps.

Site conditions

Gloved hands, low light inside open walls, fibrous material in frame.

03.05Painting & primingRoller, brush, tray, masking tape+

What we record

Masking trim, cutting in edges, rolling walls and ceilings, back rolling and touch ups.

What a model learns

Continuous coverage strokes, edge control and judging when a surface is fully coated.

Site conditions

Ladders, drop cloths, wet surfaces that change color as they dry.

SHEETA-201

Field conditions you can't stage

A robot on a jobsite meets dust, gloves and a crew that moved the ladder. Lab footage doesn't show that. Ours is recorded where the work actually happens, so these conditions come built in.

Gloved hands

Work gloves change how a grip looks and how fingers are tracked. Your model sees both.

Dust & debris

Particles in the air, residue on surfaces and lenses, scrap underfoot.

Tight spaces

Under sinks, inside walls, behind units: partial views where only one hand fits.

Changing light

From open slabs in full sun to unlit rooms lit by a work lamp.

Shared workspace

Other trades crossing the frame, handing over tools, working on the same wall.

Real tool wear

Worn bits, bent blades and taped handles. The tools a robot will actually pick up.

SHEETA-301

Capture kit

The bill of materials for every session. Video is standard. The other signals are added per project, recorded in sync with the footage.

MarkItemWhat it recordsWhy it matters on a jobsiteStatus
K-1Head-mounted cameraFirst person video from the worker's eye line, hands and tool in frame.It matches the viewpoint of a robot's own camera, which a fixed or third person angle can't do.Standard
K-2DepthDistance to surfaces and objects, aligned to each video frame.Studs, pipes and boxes sit at different depths inside the same wall.Per project
K-3Motion & pose (IMU)Head, wrist and arm movement through every step of the task.Separates a careful cut from a fast one when the video looks the same.Per project
K-4Force & tactile gloveGrip pressure and contact force at the fingertips and palm.Torque on a fitting or resistance in a drain is felt before it is seen.Per project
K-5AudioAmbient and task sound: a drill seating a screw, a cable catching in a pipe.Sound often signals that a step is complete.Per project

Resolution, frame rate and sensor configuration are set in the project spec.

SHEETA-501

The submittal

What lands in your bucket: clips with their signals, metadata and the label layers your pipeline expects. The manifest below is an illustrative example, not a client deliverable.

L-1

Step segmentation

Each clip split into named steps with start and end times: shut off, drain, disconnect, swap.

L-2

Hand pose

Keypoints for both hands, tracked through gloves and partial occlusion.

L-3

Tools & materials

Boxes and tracks for every tool and part in use, with the moment of contact marked.

L-4

Scene structure

Walls, studs, fixtures and openings labeled for spatial context.

SHEETA-801

Requests for information

The questions we usually get before a first call.

RFI-001Who records the footage?+

Working tradespeople: cleanup crews, drywallers, painters, plumbers, HVAC technicians and electricians. They record the jobs they already do, with our kit, after training and signing consent.

RFI-002Can you record a task that isn't on the list?+

Yes. The twelve tasks are where we are recording today. If your model needs another trade or a specific procedure, send the spec and we'll tell you how quickly we can staff it.

RFI-003How do you check quality?+

Every session is reviewed against the spec: framing, hands in view, task completed, signals in sync. Clips that fail are re-recorded, not delivered.

RFI-004Can we start small?+

Most clients start with a pilot on one or two tasks. It lets you test the data in your pipeline before committing to volume.

RFI-005Who owns the data?+

Rights and exclusivity are defined in each contract. Data collected for a project can be delivered for your exclusive use.

RFI-006Which labels can you deliver?+

Step segmentation, hand pose, tool and object tracking and scene structure are the most common. We follow your schema and output format.
